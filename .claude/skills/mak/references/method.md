# The method

The owner's SEO course, in our own words, one rule per section. Each rule
names the lesson it came from. When a new lesson adds to a rule, extend that
section. When a lesson brings something new, add a section with the next
number, and update the one-screen summary in SKILL.md.

The course teaches with sample businesses: an appliance-repair firm, a pet
shop, a syrup maker, a Google Ads agency. Those are **teaching material**.
Their brands, cities, phone numbers, prices and people never become content,
and never appear in this repository as facts. Use the principle, not the
example.

---

## 1. One page per search intent — the results page decides  *(lesson 2-2)*

Before two keywords share a page, search each one and read page one.

- **Same results → one page.** If "stylish shirt", "new shirt" and "shirt"
  bring up the same pages, one category page serves all three.
- **Different results → different intents → separate pages.** Four keywords
  can sound alike and still have four different page ones:
  - "Google Ads" → the service itself.
  - "Google Ads price" → dedicated pricing pages. A site without one cannot
    compete for it.
  - "Google Ads examples" → videos first. That is informational, so it goes
    in the blog.
  - "Google Ads specialist" → job boards and freelancers. The searcher wants
    to hire a person, not an agency.
- **The type of result tells you the intent:**

  | Page one shows | Intent | Where it goes |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Product, category or service pages | Commercial | A category or service page |
  | Videos, how-tos, recipes, forums | Informational | The blog tree |
  | Job boards, freelancer profiles | Hiring a person | Usually not ours |
  | One brand's own pages | Navigational | That brand's page |

**At Kestro:** `google.com` is blocked from the cloud environment. The
`WebSearch` tool returns US results, which are not Danish page one. So
evidence means the owner's screenshots of Danish page one (google.dk, logged
out), or Search Console data. Until that evidence exists, mark the decision
"to check". Do not decide on a guess.

## 2. Categories follow the buyer's first decision  *(lesson 2-2)*

- **The first level is the first choice the buyer makes.**
  - In a pet shop that is the animal: dog, cat.
  - Next to them goes a general category for what serves every animal.
  - "Pet food", "dog food" and "cat food" are three intents and get three
    pages.
- **A product may sit in many categories.**
  - Categories are cut along whatever people search by: price band, style,
    colour, feature, occasion, recipient.
  - A large shop can have more categories than products. One product may
    appear in a hundred of them.
- **Second-level groups take creativity.** Group them in the words searchers
  use: for syrups, "nutty", "spiced", "sugar-free", not one long list.
- **Thin categorisation leaves demand on the table.** A gift shop with only
  "birthday" and "Valentine's" misses every other way people search for a
  gift.
- **A category gets a page only if the site has the products.** The course's
  product sheet has an "exists on the site" tick for exactly this.

**At Kestro:** the first decision is the type of machine (bærbar, stationær,
skærm …), then the model. Possible facets:
- brand (Lenovo, Dell, HP);
- use (office, CAD);
- feature (dansk tastatur).

Each one is a candidate only. It needs a page-one check, and a real answer
Kestro can give, before it gets a page.

## 3. The blog has its own tree, and it comes second  *(lesson 2-2)*

- **An informational search never goes on a product or service category.**
  "Khakshir sherbet" shows recipes on page one: the searcher wants to make
  it, not buy it. That keyword goes in the blog.
- **The blog tree is planned as carefully as the product tree.** It has
  topics, subtopics and, under them, articles. The mocktail example runs:
  - what it is;
  - the principles of ratios;
  - the tools;
  - recipes, and under recipes: citrus, berry, tropical.
- **Order:** finish the product and service trees first, then the blog.
- **A repair business has four or five trees, not one:**
  - the repair services;
  - error codes;
  - catalogues and manuals;
  - training videos.

  Build them from the keyword pool and from how competitors categorise.

**At Kestro:**
- `/vejledninger` is the blog. `/produkter`, `/modeller`, `/ydelser` and
  `/reparation` are commercial.
- A symptom search ("bærbar larmer", "computer starter ikke") may well be
  informational. Check page one before leaving it on a repair page.

## 4. Keyword research is done per page, after the structure is final  *(lesson 2-2)*

- **One row per category in a "keyword research" sheet,** indented by level.
  Each row has empty columns for its title and description, written later.
- **Research one page's own name at a time.**
- **Take variants with the same intent:**
  - singular and plural;
  - "repair" and "repairs";
  - "authorised …";
  - "… at home", when the service can only happen at home.
- **Do not take a child page's keyword.**
  - A brand, a model or a city that has its own page owns its keyword. "Bosch
    fridge repair" does not go on "fridge repair".
  - The reverse is normal: the child's keyword contains the parent's words.
- **Do not take a different intent:**
  - "… in English" is informational.
  - "Book on …" means the searcher wants to read, so it goes to the blog or
    nowhere.
- **Harvest suggestions with autocomplete.**
  - Type the keyword plus each letter of the alphabet.
  - Then type it plus each connecting word. The course's tool draws these as
    a mind map, with the preposition branches "to", "with", "from", "in",
    "for".
  - In Danish:
    - connecting words: *til, med, fra, i, for, uden, på*;
    - question words: *hvad, hvordan, hvorfor, hvilken, hvornår*.
  - Check every suggestion against the two exclusion rules above.
- **When a modifier might change the intent, test it.** Search with and
  without it ("electric"). If page one is largely the same and most results
  do not use the modifier, it is the same intent and the same page.
- **Keep a keyword pool per category:** keyword, search volume, difficulty.
  - The course exports these from Keywordchi, a Persian tool.
  - For Danish, the volumes come from Google Keyword Planner or Search
    Console. Until the owner supplies them, they stay `unknown`, never
    estimated.

## 5. Title, description and H1 for every row  *(lesson 2-2)*

The session's homework is three things in one sheet: the pillar cluster, the
keyword research, and a title, description and H1 for every row.

- **The pillar-cluster sheet:**
  - Columns: pillar, levels 1–5, SEO title, `=LEN(title)`, SEO description,
    `=LEN(description)`.
  - Each tree is a pillar.
  - Grouping rows ("by brand", "by city", "by neighbourhood") are not pages.
    The pages sit under them.
- **The title tag** shows in the browser tab and as the headline in search.
  It is the most important on-page element.
  - 50–60 characters.
  - The target keyword at the start.
  - Attractive enough to click.
  - Unique on the site.
- **The meta description** is the summary under the title.
  - 150–160 characters.
  - The keywords used naturally, written for a person.
  - A call to action raises click-through.
  - Unique, and an accurate description of that page.
- **H1:** one per page, and part of the homework. The course has not yet
  said what goes in it. When it does, extend this rule. Until then, follow
  `references/snippets.md`.

**At Kestro the pixel limits bind:** 580 px for a title, 920 px for a
description, as Google truncates them. Characters are the course's rule of
thumb. Danish is wider than Persian, so a Danish description lands at about
145–158 characters inside 920 px.
